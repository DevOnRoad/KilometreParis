Our Story

Where it
all began.

It started with a pile of old shirts at a flea market in Cinq-Mars-la-Pile, a small village in Touraine, France. Alexandra Senes, a journalist and lifelong traveler, found herself standing before a collection of XIXth-century work shirts, simple, worn, untouched for decades. They had once belonged to a film set decorator.

His wife, who was selling them, simply wished to get rid of them, while Alexandra harbored the desire to craft a narrative of travel. Each shirt was a 'Piece Unique.' Alexandra bought them not to resell, but to embroider on. That is Kilometre Paris.

Kilometre Paris lets them become something else. Every piece carries a story — not a label, not a slogan. A place, a year, a journey. That's the only luxury we care about.

Notre-Dame de Paris · Kilometre Zero

The GPS coordinates stitched into each of our pieces teleport you to point zero: the front of the Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris, kilometre zero, where all French roads begin.


This unique feature not only grounds our creations in the heart of our Parisian heritage but also symbolizes a journey where every garment becomes a vessel for exploration and discovery.


At Kilometre Paris, we start with vintage shirts and treat them as blank canvases, handing them to artisans who embroider by hand. The point is fixed, but the embroidery tells a different story: the place it came from, the hands that made it, and the person who will wear it next.

Our Hands

Every stitch
is a
conversation.

At Kilometre Paris, every stitch we make is a conversation, between past and present, between Paris and the places our artisans call home. We work with artisans whose craft has been passed down through generations, not as a relic of the past, but as a living language.

Each piece is embroidered slowly, by hand, in a place where time moves differently. We believe that people's art is a true mirror of their minds, which makes our brand deeply human and vibrant.

The founder

Alexandra
Senes

Founder · Pilot · Traveler

Alexandra Senes is not a fashion designer in the conventional sense. She calls herself a pilot, someone who navigates between cultures, crafts, and stories.

The brand she built, Kilometre Paris, reflects that restlessness, and that respect: for the people who make, for the materials that endure, and for the places that leave a mark on us.

Our Travelers' Club

Not designers.
Translators.

Our Travelers' Club invites artists, thinkers, and creators to collaborate with us, not to design, but to translate. Using vintage shirts from the 19th century, each contributor offers their own visual interpretation of a destination that's dear to us — creating masterpiece souvenirs of our moments de vie.
